# Launch-day visual assets

These raster plates are production inputs for the approved reduced 16-bit launch-day direction.
They are visual translations, never historical evidence.

## Files

- `pad39a-establishing-snes.png` — generated at 1672 × 941 and reduced with nearest-neighbor
  sampling to the game's 320 × 180 logical canvas.
- `armstrong-window-exterior-snes.png` — generated as a separate continuous exterior layer,
  corrected from sunrise to approximately 09:32 EDT lighting, then reduced to 480 × 270 so Phaser
  can translate and scale it behind the commander's window mask.

Both final assets were generated with the built-in image-generation tool on 22 August 2026. No
generated detail may be used to settle spacecraft configuration, operator ownership, timing or
launch-site geometry. Those decisions remain governed by `CAMPAIGN.md` and
`docs/campaign/01-launch-day.md`.

## Final prompt set

### Pad 39A establishing plate

> A museum-quality 16-bit pixel-art establishing view of Apollo 11 Saturn V AS-506 on Pad 39A on
> the morning of 16 July 1969. The Saturn V and red launch umbilical tower dominate the right half;
> a transfer van and three tiny suited figures establish human scale on the left. Late-SNES pixel
> clusters, restrained 32–48-color navy, blue-gray, coral, aged-white, charcoal and oxidized-red
> palette, controlled dithering, early Florida daylight and subtle venting vapor. Environment plate
> only: no UI, captions, logos, readable text or watermark; no vector blockout, modern objects,
> science-fiction additions or neon.

### Armstrong window exterior

> A late-SNES pixel-art exterior layer from inside Apollo 11 Command Module Columbia, looking from
> Neil Armstrong's left-hand window toward Pad 39A during the final 20 seconds before liftoff. A
> close red tower lattice occupies the right third; the pad, coastline, flat Florida horizon and
> bright approximately 09:32 EDT morning sky extend below and left. Preserve enough continuous sky
> and ground for Phaser to move the layer vertically during liftoff. Exterior only: no cockpit,
> astronaut, rocket, UI, captions, logos, text or watermark; no third-person camera, sci-fi geometry
> or sunset lighting.
